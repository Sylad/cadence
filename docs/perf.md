# perf

`cadence perf [folder] [--since date] [--json]` is the permanent dashboard of the workflow. It calls no model, writes nothing
(no journal, no plan, no cache) and reads, from the folder (default: the current one), the wave journals
`<folder>/.cadence/runs/<wave>/<project>--<lot>.json` (with each wave's `wave.json`) and the plan of every project of the folder
(one nesting level, like `cadence corrections`: `group/gamma`; a `tmp/` folder is never read: its working copies would count their lots twice, L241). Exit code `0`, even when a `⚠` is printed; `1` on a usage or read error.

It compares two windows of 7 calendar days (local days): the **current** one (the last 7 days, today included) and the **previous**
one (the 7 days before). Every speed indicator is printed on the same line as its **quality twin**, never alone ("fast *and* well"):

| Family | Indicator | Twin | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Throughput | lots ready, lots handed back per day, lots delivered (plan `done` with `finished` in the window), share of handed-back lots within their token budget | escaped defects | is the flow dropping? look for the bottleneck before adding lots; are the budgets right? |
| Corrections | median of `fix` passes per lot, share of lots with 2 passes or more | share of lots right the first time (no `fix` pass) | is the first write good enough? harden the sheet or the brief |
| Corrections | dominant causes (top 3 of the classified passes, the unclassified share apart) | — | which link to fix |
| Lead interventions | lots handed back by a wave then finished outside it, sessions that see the application per visible lot (`ux` steps, plus the `review-small` of a small visible lot), share of visible lots reviewed by a dedicated `ux` step | escaped defects (sessions per lot only) | does the wave stop too early? does the ergonomics review cost more than one round? |

A line starts with `⚠` and ends with the decision it informs when its value is **worse than the previous window by more than 25 %**
(a drop for the throughput and the share right the first time; a rise for passes, interventions and escaped defects). No `⚠`, and the line says
why, when one window holds fewer than 5 lots for the indicator (10 steps for a wall-time duration, L280), when the previous value is zero, or when `--since` truncates a window.
A window with no lot for an indicator (no rendered lot for ready lots, no delivered lot for delivered, none at all for a window entirely before `--since`) gives `n/d` (`null` in JSON), never `0`. A **clear bottleneck** (one cause weighing at least half of the classified passes of the
current window, with at least 10 classified passes) is flagged by a `⚠` even without a trend.

- **Dating.** A journal is dated by the end of its last step (else by the creation of its wave); a journal without a readable date is left out. A correction pass is dated
  by the end of its `fix` step. The classification of a pass is `cadence corrections`' (same reading, `src/corrections.ts`): without finding to read it is `sans-constat`,
  otherwise the cause of `<folder>/.cadence/corrections.json` (a manual cause first), `non-classe` when absent from it. `perf` never classifies: run `cadence corrections --classify`, which the cause line recalls as soon as a pass with findings is unclassified. Without any model, the line below it, `origine des passes à constats`, counts the passes of the window by the kind of review that raised them (`review`, `review-small`, `ux`), read in the journals (`corrections.causes.<window>.byReview` in `--json`) (L240).
- **Escaped defects** = distinct lots with a plan note dated in the window, written after the lot was done, that starts with `QA` (after the `DD-MM` date prefix) and
  contains `bloquant` or `majeur` not preceded by `0` or `aucun`. Until a plan holds such a note the value is `n/d` ("aucune note QA reconnue : convention à fixer (L227)"). Reopened lots are not counted: the plan does not date a reopening.
- **Lead interventions.** A lot counts when its last journal (all waves of the folder) is `handed-back` or `failed` and its plan lot is `done` with `finished` on or after the day it was handed back.
  A journal joins its plan by the project name (the path of the project relative to the folder) and the `lot` field of the journal; an id `Q4/accueil-4-ux12` joins the plan lot `Q4`. Journals with no plan lot are counted aside (`N lot(s) de journal sans plan`) and never counted as finished outside the wave.
  An unreadable plan skips its project with a warning on the error output and the other projects are read; the indicators that read the plans are `n/d` only when no plan is readable.
- **Sessions that see the application.** `sessions ux par lot visible` counts, per visible lot rendered in the window, its `ux` steps that ended `ok` **and** the `review-small` steps that ended `ok` of a small visible lot (journal `small: true`): the single pass of a small visible lot is its only ergonomics review, run with the application launched (L283; measured on 24-10, 139 of 140 small lots were visible and the line showed 0.0, −97 %, a false signal). The `review-small` of a lot that is not small is only a code review and is not counted; a journal written before the `small` field counts none.
  `part des lots visibles revus par une étape ux` is the share of the visible lots rendered in the window with at least one `ux` step that ended `ok` (so a small visible lot, reviewed in `review-small`, is not in it). It is informational: no `⚠`, no quality twin (`interventions.dedicatedUx` in `--json`), `n/d` without a visible lot.
- **Pre-check share of `oui`** (L288). `part de « oui » du précheck` is the share of the lots rendered in the window whose journal records a pre-check verdict (`precheckVerdict`) that is `oui` (the deliverable was already there; an unproven `oui` is recorded `partiel`). Compare the current window with the previous one before and after a change of `orchestrate.precheck` or of the pre-check criterion (the title rule of L288): a share that stays low means the pre-check costs more than it saves. A journal older than L288 has no `precheckVerdict`: its verdict is read back from the lot (`livrable déjà présent` → `oui`, pre-check finding in the brief → `partiel`, otherwise `non`), so the window before the change is measurable. A journal written since L288 whose pre-check report was unreadable (it keeps the `contrôle préalable illisible` warning, no `precheckVerdict`) has no verdict and is not counted, not read back as `non` (L293). Lots with no successful `precheck` step are not counted; informational, no `⚠`, no quality twin (`precheck.yes` in `--json`), `n/d` without a verdict.
- **Pre-check measured per lot** (L322). Under the section « Contrôle préalable », for the current window: the split `oui` / `partiel` / `non` of the rendered lots (`précheck : oui 1 · partiel 0 · non 1`); the local sessions (`précheck local : n sessions, k échecs, m min`: how many, how many ended without a usable report, wall time; a local session that ended `ok` but whose report was unreadable, so that the lot fell back on Sonnet, counts as a failure, and its line says `local échec`, `--json` `local.status: "illisible"`); the Sonnet sessions of the pre-check, which are the fallback of a local failure and the confirmation of a local `oui` (`précheck Sonnet : n sessions, t k tokens, m min`; the local model counts no token); the gain of the `oui` (`gain des « oui » : n sessions d'implémentation évitées (≈ t k tokens)`: the lots handed back without implementation, times the median of the `implement` steps of the window; without any, the tokens read `(gain en tokens : n/d)` and are `null` in `--json`); then one line per lot (`projet:lot · verdict · local échec 10 min · Sonnet 2 min, 20 k tokens`). Nothing is printed for a window without pre-check session or verdict. In `--json`: `precheck.verdicts`, `precheck.cost` (`local`, `sonnet`, `avoided`, each `{ current, previous }` as a whole) and `precheck.lots` (current window). Read it before changing `orchestrate.precheck` or the lots it applies to: the cost of the pre-check is what it spends (`local` minutes, `sonnet` tokens), its worth is `avoided`.
- **Share within budget** (L349). `part des rendus sur budget` counts a lot against its budget **minus the share kept for the escalation** (`escalationReserve` of the journal, `docs/orchestrate.md`) as long as its escalation was not engaged: the ordinary passes stop that share earlier, so counting them against the whole budget would call a lot that hit its limit « within budget ». Once the escalation is engaged (a journal `escalation` whose issue is not `non jouée`) the share is released at run time, so all the steps of the lot, whatever their model, are counted against the whole budget. An escalation `non jouée` engaged nothing: the ordinary passes ate the share, so the lot stays counted against its budget minus the share. A journal with no `escalationReserve` (a wave before L349, or no `orchestrate.escalation`) is counted against its whole budget, as before.
- **Model escalations** (L337). Under the section « Escalade de modèle », for the current window: `escalades de modèle : n lots · k conforme · r rendu malgré l'escalade · b non jouée · e en échec` — the finished lots whose journal carries an `escalation` (`orchestrate.escalation`, `docs/orchestrate.md`) and how it ended: the fresh review on the escalation model was compliant (`conforme`), the lot was handed back despite it (`rendu`), the lot's budget did not cover it (`non jouée`), or an escalated session returned nothing — model unavailable to the account, cut, timeout (`en échec`, the model never judged the lot); a lot interrupted during its escalation, withdrawn from the wave (`--drop`) or stopped by an incident of the repository is counted among the lots but in no outcome, never as `en échec`. Nothing is printed without an escalation. In `--json`: `escalations` (`{ current, previous }`, each `{ lots, conforme, rendu, nonJouee, echec }`). Read it to see whether the escalation model earns its cost: many `rendu` means the stronger model does not settle what the passes could not, so the brief or the sheet is at fault.

- **Tests unstable under load** (L354). Under the section « Porte de tests sous charge », for the current window: `tests instables sous charge : n tests dans k lots (rouges dans la suite, verts rejoués seuls ; précédente m)` — the tests of the rendered lots that were red in the gate's whole suite and green when the gate replayed them alone (`instables` of the lot journal, only for a project that declares `orchestrate.testReplay`, `docs/orchestrate.md`): they were neither counted nor corrected. Nothing is printed without one. In `--json`: `instables` (`{ current, previous }`, each `{ lots, tests }`). A rise says the machine is too loaded for the suite: lower the session cap or the `CADENCE_TEST_WORKERS` share before reading it as flaky tests.
- **`--since date`** is an ISO date that ignores everything before that day in both windows (a wave created before that day is not read, even if its lots end after it); a window starting before it is *truncated* (its per-day rates divide by the days covered, no `⚠`).
  It is **not** the instant of `cadence corrections --since` (waves created strictly after).
- **`--json`** prints one object `{ since, today, windows: { current, previous }, throughput, corrections, interventions, precheck, escalations, instables, tokens, delays, durations, cost, quality, orphans, warnings }`; every indicator carries
  `{ current, previous, change, warn, twin, lots, note }` (`twin`: the path of its quality twin, e.g. `throughput.escaped`). Same values as the text.

## Tokens, delays, wall time and cost (L226)

The same two windows, the same `⚠` (more than 25 % worse, at least 5 lots in each window, or 10 steps for a wall-time duration (L280: under 10 steps the change from one window to the next cannot be interpreted, `docs/perf/recalage-2026-10-24.md`), previous value not zero, no truncated window) and the same
quality twin (the share of lots right the first time) on the same line. A rise is the worse direction for each of them.

| Indicator | Reading | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| tokens per lot, by estimate | median of the counted tokens (sum of `tokens.counted` of every step) of the rendered journals of the window whose lot joins the plan, one class per exact estimate (`0.5`, `1`, `2`…), a lot that went through the spec chain (a `spec`, `fiche`, `maquette` or `accept` step; 450 k is the gap of their *budget* (`SPEC_STAGE_ALLOCATION`), the measured gap is smaller, about 150 k at estimate 0.5 and 30 k at estimate 1 on 12–18-10, `docs/perf/goulot-2026-10-18.md`; on 17–23-10 the `⚠` on estimates 0.25 and 0.5 (+27 %) came with a share right the first time up from 37 to 59 %, so the reading is « heavier first write, fewer corrections », `docs/perf/recalage-2026-10-24.md`, L280) in its own class `0.5+spec`, printed after `0.5`, so that a change in the mix of lots is not read as a heavier ordinary lot (L240); a failed lot counts, a journal with no readable tokens or without plan lot does not. Each class also carries `carrier` in the JSON (L312): the kind of step whose tokens per lot, averaged over the lots of the class, rise most from one window to the other (`review` and `review-small` count as one step, the size of the lot picks one or the other; `{ kind, delta }`; `null` when the total per lot does not rise on average) | is the estimate calibrated? redo the split or the budgets. **The advice names the step** (L312): « la hausse est portée par l'étape `implement` (+20 k par lot) », and when the share right the first time rose in the same window the line reads « de la qualité payée, pas une estimate fausse ; juger cette étape (modèle, effort, passes) avant de recaler l'estimate » instead of « l'estimate est mal calibrée » (17–24-10 against 10–17-10: `implement` carries the rise at estimates 0.25 and 0.5, `docs/perf/recalage-2026-10-24-soir.md`) |
| share of review tokens | counted tokens of the steps whose kind starts with `review` or is `ux`, over the counted tokens of all steps of the rendered journals; a ratio, `n/d` without journal | does the review weigh too much against the writing? lighten it (`orchestrate.review.threshold`) |
| delay created → done | median of `finished − created` (calendar days) of the plan lots `done` with `finished` in the window and both dates readable | is the queue growing? |
| delay done → delivery | median of the days between `finished` and the first line of the repository's `deliveries.log` on or after it (`<git common dir>/cadence/`, one repository per project, worktrees included; read without creating anything); `n/d` for a project that is not a git repository, has no log or no later line | deliver in smaller batches? |
| wall time per step kind | median of `ended − started` in minutes of the `ok` steps with both bounds readable, ranked in the window by the local day of `ended`; one line per kind seen | which step is slow? timeouts, effort or model |
| cost per delivered lot | median in euros of the sum, over every step of every journal of the lot (all waves), of the step cost; lots `done` in the window with at least one journal | is the model chosen for each step right? |

**Price grid.** Declared in the `cadence.yaml` of the folder, euros per million tokens, the three prices mandatory for a declared model:

```yaml
perf:
  prices:
    sonnet: { input: 3, output: 15, cacheRead: 0.3 }
    opus: { input: 15, output: 75, cacheRead: 1.5 }
    haiku: { input: 1, output: 5, cacheRead: 0.1 }
```

An unknown key (under `perf`, under a model, or a model other than `sonnet`, `opus`, `haiku`), a negative or non-numeric price, or an unreadable file refuses the command before any journal is read (`raf: …`, exit 1, no output).
Without `perf.prices` (absent or empty) the cost is `n/d` and a warning says where to declare it; the other indicators are served.

**Cost of a step** = (`input` + `cacheWrite`) × input price + `output` × output price + `cacheRead` × cacheRead price, per million tokens, at the price of the step's own model. Assumptions written in the sheet (`docs/specs/perf-tokens-delais-cout/fiche.yaml`, R8):
cache writes are billed at the input price (the grid has no write price, so the cost is slightly underestimated if they are billed more); a step that only carries `counted` (an old journal) is split 90 % input, 10 % output, no cache read;
a step without any readable token costs 0 € (the lot stays in the population); a `local` step costs 0 € whatever its counters (outside quota and budget) and needs no price.
A lot with a step of a model that has no price in the grid (or no readable `model`, named `inconnu`) is left out of the cost and a warning on the error output names the model and the number of lots; delivered lots without any journal are counted aside (`N lot(s) livré(s) sans journal`). No `⚠` on a cost that is `n/d`.

No indicator of produced volume (lines of code, commits, total tokens or euros of a window) is printed or exposed: only per-lot medians, median durations and shares come out. Tokens and euros compare lots of the same size; they never set a spending target.

## Delivery quality and the tour header (L227)

`cadence deliver` appends one line `day sha [class] reason ⏎ line ⏎ line` to `<git common dir>/cadence/deliveries-failed.log` for every failure after the lock is taken (script, CI, deploy or check; the reason is the first line of the failure message, the class one of `refus`, `ci`, `deploiement`, `verification`, and up to 5 bounded lines of the failing command's output follow, see [deliver](deliver.md)). A green delivery or a refusal before the lock writes nothing there; `deliveries.log` is unchanged. A failed write of that log leaves the exit code and the failure message of the delivery as they are and adds the warning `deliver : journal des livraisons rouges non écrit : <cause>` on the error output. An exception or a crash of `deliver` writes no line.

| Indicator | Reading | Twin | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| red deliveries | lines of every project's `deliveries-failed.log` dated in the window; a repository without the file counts `0` (not `n/d`), an unreadable line, or an unreadable file, counts nothing; printed on the « livrés » line | delivered lots | the delivery breaks: fix CI, deploy or checks before adding lots |
| blocking QA findings | plan notes dated in the window, on a lot already `done` at the note's date, that start with `QA` and contain `bloquant` not preceded by `0` or `aucun` (`majeur` alone does not count); counted note by note, unlike the escaped defects; `n/d` until a note is recognised; printed on the escaped-defects line | escaped defects | the review lets serious defects through: harden the review before delivering more |

Both are judged like the other indicators (`⚠` above +25 %). For red deliveries the sample is the deliveries of the window, green and red (at least 5 per window); for blocking QA findings it is the plan lots `done` in the window (at least 5 per window). `--json` adds `quality: { redDeliveries, qaBlocking }` (Indicator format, `twin` `throughput.delivered` and `throughput.escaped`); every other key is unchanged, and `throughput.delivered.twin` stays `throughput.escaped`.

**Red deliveries by class and project** (L282): when the current window holds at least one red delivery, `cadence perf` prints under the « Débit » section two lines, `livraisons rouges par classe (fenêtre courante)` and `livraisons rouges par projet`, each sorted by count (ties: refus, CI, déploiement, vérification, then `non classée` for lines whose class is unknown). `--json` adds `quality.reds: { current, previous }`, each `{ count, byClass: { refus, ci, deploiement, verification, inconnue }, byProject: { <project>: { <class>: n } } }`; the other keys are unchanged. **Lines written before the class** (L331): a line with no `[class]` is classed from its wording, the one `deliver` writes itself — `script de livraison en échec (code 2)` → refus, any other code (or a timeout) → déploiement, `déploiement en échec` → déploiement, `CADENCE_DEPLOYED_FILE …` (declared sha invalid or unreadable) → déploiement, `CI en échec` / `CI non terminée après N s` / `gh run list en échec` / `aucun run CI pour ce sha après N min` → ci, `vérification en échec` → verification; the wording must start the reason. Any other line stays `non classée`. The deduction happens at read time: `deliveries-failed.log` is never rewritten. The reason itself (the last lines of the failing command's output) stays in `deliveries-failed.log`: read it there.

`cadence lead tour` prints the `⚠` lines of `perf` as its **perf header** (see [lead tour](lead.md#lead-tour)).
